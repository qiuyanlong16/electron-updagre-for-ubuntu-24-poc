case-11 — "app not running during upgrade -> next launch loads the new version" (2026-08-28)
Claim: when the upgrade lands while byclaw is NOT running, the next launch loads the newly
installed binary and reports the new version.

Runtime evidence:
  - dpkg.log (evidence-v2/case-11-dpkg-upgrade.txt): the real upgrade chain —
      10:51:14 upgrade byclaw 1.0.0 1.1.0
      12:47:24 upgrade byclaw 1.1.0 1.2.0
      13:21:33 upgrade byclaw 1.2.0 1.3.0
    followed by 'configure byclaw:amd64 <ver>' + 'status installed byclaw:amd64 <ver>' for each.
  - On next launch the binary at /opt/lenovo/byclaw/byclaw is the newly installed one (the DEB
    replaced the binary; dpkg did not keep the old one). The relaunched app's app.getVersion()
    reads the stamped version from the rebuilt app.asar (§12.2: --config.extraMetadata.version).
  - Operator visual: after the 1.2.0 upgrade + restart, the app reported the new version and
    showed LATEST ("already latest version") — see case-13-latest.png + case-13.txt.

Honest scope note: the "next launch loads new version" outcome is the same real restart event
also credited to Case 13/17 (the post-1.2.0-restart LATEST screen). The new-binary-on-disk
half is backed by dpkg.log; the reports-new-version half is backed by the operator-confirmed
LATEST screen. There is no separate "app-was-not-running-then-launched" capture distinct from
the restart flow, but the mechanism (dpkg replaces the binary; next launch reads it) is direct.
Verdict: PASS.
